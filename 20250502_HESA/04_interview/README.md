# Insights from The University of Manchester's Access and Participation Plan (2020-21 to 2024-25)

## 1. Performance Assessment Overview
The University of Manchester's performance evaluation draws on internal, external, sector-level comparator data, and benchmarks against English Russell Group institutions. It focuses on key dimensions such as access, continuation, attainment, and progression across diverse student groups, revealing both progress and persistent gaps.

## 2. Key Student Group Insights
### 2.1 Socioeconomic Background (POLAR4, Household Income, NS-SEC)
- **Access**: In 2017/18, 7.8% of full-time undergraduates were from POLAR4 Quintile 1 (low-participation neighbourhoods, LPNs), a 1.2% increase over five years. However, this is still below the sector average of 12%. The ratio of Quintile 5 (high-participation) to Quintile 1 entrants fell from 6.3:1 to 5.2:1. Around 25% of intake came from low socio-economic backgrounds (NS-SEC 4-7), up 4% in five years, and 29.9% from low-income households (below £25k), a stable proportion.
- **Continuation**: LPN students had a higher continuation rate (94%) than the sector (89.2%) in 2016/17. Low-income and low-SE background students saw improvements (92.2%→94.5% and 92.1%→92.7% respectively), with small gaps (2.2% and 1.3%) against non-widening participation (WP) peers. A critical finding: 24% of Q1 students lived at home in first year (vs. 7% of Q5), with a continuation rate of 87.5% (vs. 95% for those in university accommodation).
- **Attainment**: The attainment gap between Q1 and Q5 students halved to 4% over five years (8% increase for Q1 vs. 4.2% for Q5). Entry tariff score is the strongest predictor of good degrees—students with 128-143 tariff points (ABB-AAA) had similar attainment (87% for Q1 vs. 85% for Q5). Low-income students had a persistent 6% attainment gap, with variations across schools (2.2% in Mathematics to 11.6% in Materials).
- **Progression**: No significant differences in employment/further study by POLAR4 quintile. Low-SE background students outperformed peers in 2016/17 (81.2% vs. 80.0% in graduate-level roles).

### 2.2 Black, Asian, and Minority Ethnic (BAME) Students
- **Access**: BAME undergraduate participation rose from 23.3% to 30.2% over five years. The university has a higher BAME proportion than Russell Group peers (27.6% vs. 21.3% in 2016/17) but underrepresents Black students (3.8% vs. 3.6% population estimate) and overrepresents Asian students. BAME students from deprived areas (lowest two IMD quintiles) saw a 5.2% participation increase, outpacing the sector’s 3.8%.
- **Continuation**: Asian students had the highest continuation rate (98%), but Bangladeshi/Pakistani students (60% living at home) had lower rates. White students from deprived areas had the lowest rate (92%), but all groups outperformed the sector.
- **Attainment**: Black students’ attainment increased by 16% (vs. 6% for White students), narrowing the gap to 11.6% (not statistically significant). The sector’s Black-White gap remains larger (23.1%). The Asian-White gap is 10.6%, with slower improvement. Low-tariff BAME students (below 162.5) face larger gaps (13.6% for Asian, 9.8% for Black vs. White peers).
- **Progression**: No significant differences in employment/further study, but BAME students are more likely to be unemployed. TEF metrics flagged low employment for Black/Asian students against sector benchmarks.

### 2.3 Mature Students (21+)
- **Access**: Mature student proportion fell from 9% to 7.9% (12 young students per mature student in 2017/18), contrasting with the sector’s 3.1% increase. Most are 21-25, focused on Health/Medical courses. Part-time mature enrollment (mostly Nursing CPD) dropped from 345 to 145.
- **Continuation**: Mature students have lower continuation rates (92% vs. 96.2% for young students), but the gap halved and is no longer significant. The university outperforms the sector (sector gap: 7%).
- **Attainment**: Mature students’ attainment increased by 12% (vs. 5.1% for young students), narrowing the gap. Those with prior first degrees had the highest attainment (84%), while Access course graduates had the lowest (69%).
- **Progression**: Mature students are more likely to progress to work/study (85% vs. 75.7% for young students) and perform above sector benchmarks in post-graduation salaries.

### 2.4 Disabled Students
- **Access**: Disabled student proportion rose by 4.5%, driven by mental health disabilities (1.5%→5%). The sector saw a 3% rise, with mental health disabilities at 4%.
- **Continuation**: Rates peaked in 2014/15 then fell; mental health disabled students have a 9% gap (87% vs. non-disabled peers). Those without Disabled Student Allowance (DSA) have the poorest continuation (30% of mental health disabled students receive DSA).
- **Attainment**: Attainment increased by 7%, but the gap only reduced by 1%. Mental health disabled students saw a 16% increase, with a 5% gap in 2017/18. The sector’s gap is smaller (3%).
- **Progression**: The gap narrowed from 7.2% (2013/14) to 3.1% (2016/17). Disabled students with 144-159 tariff points had lower good degree rates (80.1% vs. 86.5% for non-disabled peers).

### 2.5 Care Leavers
- Small cohort sizes limit meaningful comparisons. 79% of care leavers continued to second year (below university average), and 66.7% obtained 1st/2:1 degrees (below average).

### 2.6 Intersections of Disadvantage
- LPN status correlates with low income (44% of Q1 students) and low SE background (42%).
- BAME groups (Bangladeshi, Pakistani, Black, Chinese) are overrepresented in WP backgrounds; Indian/White students are underrepresented.
- Over 60% of Pakistani/Bangladeshi students live at home (linked to lower continuation).
- 72% of students with mental health disabilities are female; mature students are more likely to report disabilities.

## 3. Strategic Aims and Targets
The university’s strategy is rooted in a "Theory of Change" and three core goals: world-class research, outstanding learning experience, and social responsibility. Key targets include:
- **Access**: Reduce the Q5:Q1 POLAR4 ratio from 5.2:1 to 3:1 by 2025 via contextual offers, Access Manchester initiatives, and partnerships (e.g., Greater Manchester Higher).
- **Attainment**: Eliminate unexplained gaps between White-Black, White-Asian, disabled-non-disabled, and IMD Q1-Q5 students by 2024/25.
- **Progression**: Close unemployment gaps between White and Black students by 2024/25; enhance careers support for low-income, BAME, and disabled students.

## 4. Strategic Measures
### 4.1 Access
- Pre-16: Collaborate with partners (e.g., The Brilliant Club) on outreach, Maths Attainment Raising Strategy, and IntoUniversity Centre.
- Post-16: Expand Access Manchester (contextual offers, targeted support for Black/White LPN students) and sign the Stand Alone Pledge for estranged students.
- Sanctuary: Offer Article 26 Scholarships for sanctuary seekers; open libraries to asylum seekers.

### 4.2 Student Success
- Learning Support: Scale My Learning Essentials (MLE) and Peer Support (PASS) programs, with a focus on commuter students (timetabling adjustments).
- Teaching Innovation: Establish the Manchester Institute of Teaching and Learning (MITL) and Curriculum Evolution project (inclusion, employability, wellbeing).
- Mental Health: Launch a 2-year Greater Manchester Student Mental Health Hub (2019) and in-house Specialist Mentor provision for mental health/social communication disabilities.
- Financial Support: Manchester Bursary (£2k/year for income <£25k, £1k for £25k-£35k) and hardship funds for care leavers/estranged students.

### 4.3 Progression
- Careers Support: Work Experience Bursaries (up to £1k), Student Experience Internships (disabled students), Manchester Gold mentoring, and Global Graduates program.
- Postgraduate Access: Introduce Manchester Master’s Bursary (£3k) and embed OfS Addressing Barriers project outcomes.

## 5. Student Consultation and Governance
- Strong partnership with the Students’ Union (UMSU): SU representation on key committees (APSG, PRC) and co-production of initiatives (e.g., Diversity and Inclusion Student Ambassadors).
- Focus groups with diverse students to shape strategy; plans to improve LGBTQ+ student data and support.

## 6. Evaluation and Monitoring
- Oversight by the Access and Participation Monitoring and Evaluation (APME) Sub-group.
- Commitment to theory of change for core programs, data linkage (e.g., graduate outcomes to activities), and longitudinal tracking via HEAT.
- Annual Widening Participation Report and integration of APP targets into the university’s Annual Performance Review (APR).

## 7. Financial Investment
- Total investment (access, financial support, research/evaluation) remains ~25% of Higher Fee Income (HFI) annually (2020-25).
- Key allocations: £11.68M (2020-21) for financial support, £3.53M for access activities, and £875k for research/evaluation.

## 8. Key Challenges and Opportunities
- **Challenges**: Persistent attainment gaps for BAME/disabled/low-income students; small cohort sizes (e.g., care leavers, Black students) leading to volatile data; commuter students’ lower continuation; mental health support gaps.
- **Opportunities**: Collaborative regional initiatives (GM Mental Health Hub, GM Higher); targeted programs for intersectional groups; curriculum reform and digital innovation (My Manchester platforms); strong SU partnership and student co-creation.

# 曼彻斯特大学入学与参与计划（2020-21 至 2024-25 学年）核心洞察
## 一、绩效评估概述
曼彻斯特大学的绩效评估基于内部、外部数据、行业对标数据，并以英国罗素集团高校为基准。评估聚焦不同学生群体在入学机会、学业延续率、学业成就及毕业发展等关键维度的表现，既展现了已取得的进展，也揭示了仍存在的差距。

## 二、重点学生群体洞察
### （一）社会经济背景相关群体（基于 POLAR4 分区、家庭收入、社会经济地位分类）
- **入学机会**：2017/18 学年，7.8% 的全日制本科生来自 POLAR4 第一五分位（低高等教育参与社区，LPNs），五年间增长 1.2%，但仍低于全国高校平均水平（12%）。第五五分位（高参与社区）与第一五分位学生的入学比例从 6.3:1 降至 5.2:1。约 25% 的新生来自低社会经济地位背景（NS-SEC 4-7 类），五年间增长 4%；29.9% 来自低收入家庭（年收入低于 2.5 万英镑），这一比例近年保持稳定。
- **学业延续率**：2016/17 学年，低参与社区学生的学业延续率（94%）高于全国高校平均水平（89.2%）。低收入和低社会经济地位学生的延续率均有提升（分别从 92.2% 升至 94.5%、从 92.1% 升至 92.7%），与非拓展参与（WP）群体的差距较小（分别为 2.2% 和 1.3%）。关键发现：24% 的第一五分位学生第一学年居住在父母家中（第五五分位仅 7%），其学业延续率仅为 87.5%（而无论 POLAR 分区如何，居住在大学宿舍的学生延续率达 95%）。
- **学业成就**：五年间，第一五分位与第五五分位学生的学业成就差距缩小一半至 4%（第一五分位增长 8%，第五五分位增长 4.2%）。入学成绩是获得优异学位的最强预测因素——入学 tariff 分数在 128-143 分（相当于 ABB-AAA 等级）的学生，无论 POLAR 分区如何，学业成就相近（第一五分位 87%，第五五分位 85%）。低收入学生的学业成就差距持续保持在 6%，且不同学院间差异显著（数学学院 2.2%，材料学院 11.6%）。
- **毕业发展**：不同 POLAR4 分区学生在就业或深造方面无显著差异。2016/17 学年，低社会经济地位学生的毕业发展表现优于同龄人（81.2% 进入研究生阶段工作或学习，同龄人为 80.0%）。

### （二）黑人、亚裔及少数族裔（BAME）学生
- **入学机会**：五年间，少数族裔本科生比例从 23.3% 升至 30.2%。该校少数族裔学生比例高于罗素集团同类高校（2016/17 学年为 27.6%，同类高校平均 21.3%），但黑人学生比例偏低（3.8%，略高于人口统计估算的 3.6%），亚裔学生比例偏高。来自贫困地区（IMD 最低两个五分位）的少数族裔学生入学比例增长 5.2%，高于全国高校 3.8% 的增长水平。
- **学业延续率**：亚裔学生延续率最高（98%），但孟加拉裔和巴基斯坦裔学生（60% 居住在父母家中）延续率较低。来自贫困地区的白人学生延续率最低（92%），但所有群体表现均优于全国高校平均水平。
- **学业成就**：黑人学生学业成就增长 16%（白人学生增长 6%），差距缩小至 11.6%（无统计学显著性）。全国高校黑人与白人学生的成就差距仍更大（23.1%）。亚裔与白人学生的成就差距为 10.6%，改善速度较慢。低 tariff 分数（低于 162.5 分）的少数族裔学生面临更大差距（亚裔与白人差距 13.6%，黑人与白人差距 9.8%）。
- **毕业发展**：不同族裔学生在就业或深造方面无显著差异，但少数族裔学生失业概率更高。教学卓越框架（TEF）数据显示，该校黑人与亚裔学生的就业率低于全国高校加权基准，被标记为显著负面指标。

### （三）成年学生（21 岁及以上）
- **入学机会**：成年学生比例从 9% 降至 7.9%（2017/18 学年每 12 名年轻学生对应 1 名成年学生），与全国高校 3.1% 的增长趋势相反。大多数成年学生年龄在 21-25 岁，集中在健康与医学相关专业。非全日制成年学生（主要为护理专业持续教育）入学人数从 345 人降至 145 人。
- **学业延续率**：成年学生延续率低于年轻学生（92% 对比 96.2%），但差距缩小一半且不再具有统计学显著性。该校成年学生延续率表现优于全国高校（全国高校差距为 7%）。
- **学业成就**：成年学生学业成就增长 12%（年轻学生增长 5.1%），差距逐步缩小。拥有先前学士学位的成年学生成就最高（84%），而通过高等教育衔接课程（Access course）入学的学生成就最低（69%）。
- **毕业发展**：成年学生毕业后更易进入工作或深造（85% 对比年轻学生的 75.7%），且毕业后三年的薪资水平高于全国高校基准。

### （四）残疾学生
- **入学机会**：残疾学生比例增长 4.5%，主要受心理健康障碍学生增长推动（从 1.5% 升至 5%）。全国高校残疾学生比例增长 3%，心理健康障碍学生比例为 4%。
- **学业延续率**：延续率在 2014/15 学年达到峰值后下降；心理健康障碍学生与非残疾学生的延续率差距为 9%（87% 对比非残疾学生）。未获得残疾学生津贴（DSA）的学生延续率最低（仅 30% 的心理健康障碍学生获得 DSA）。
- **学业成就**：五年间残疾学生学业成就增长 7%，但差距仅缩小 1%。心理健康障碍学生成就增长 16%，2017/18 学年与非残疾学生的差距为 5%。全国高校残疾与非残疾学生的成就差距更小（3%）。
- **毕业发展**：差距从 2013/14 学年的 7.2% 缩小至 2016/17 学年的 3.1%。tariff 分数在 144-159 分的残疾学生获得优异学位的比例低于非残疾学生（80.1% 对比 86.5%）。

### （五）脱离监护学生（Care Leavers）
- 该群体规模较小，难以进行有意义的对比分析。79% 的脱离监护学生升入第二学年（低于全校平均水平），66.7% 获得一等或二等一学位（低于全校平均水平）。

### （六）弱势因素的交叉影响
- 低参与社区（LPN）身份与低收入（44% 的第一五分位学生）、低社会经济地位（42%）高度相关。
- 少数族裔群体（孟加拉裔、巴基斯坦裔、黑人、华裔）在拓展参与（WP）背景学生中比例偏高；印度裔和白人学生在 WP 群体中比例偏低。
- 60% 以上的巴基斯坦裔和孟加拉裔学生第一学年居住在父母家中（与较低的延续率相关）。
- 72% 的心理健康障碍学生为女性；成年学生更可能报告残疾情况。

## 三、战略目标
该校战略基于“变革理论”，并围绕三大核心目标：世界级研究、卓越学习体验和社会责任。核心目标包括：
- **入学机会**：通过背景化录取、“曼彻斯特入学计划”（Access Manchester）及区域合作（如大曼彻斯特高等教育联盟），到 2025 年将 POLAR4 第五五分位与第一五分位学生的入学比例从 5.2:1 降至 3:1。
- **学业成就**：到 2024/25 学年，消除白人与黑人、白人与亚裔、残疾与非残疾、IMD 第一五分位与第五五分位学生之间无法解释的学业成就差距。
- **毕业发展**：到 2024/25 学年，缩小白人与黑人学生的失业率差距；加强对低收入、少数族裔和残疾学生的就业支持。

## 四、战略举措
### （一）入学机会拓展
- 16 岁前阶段：与合作伙伴（如“卓越俱乐部”）开展拓展项目、数学成绩提升战略，并通过“大学入门中心”（IntoUniversity Centre）提供支持。
- 16 岁后阶段：扩大“曼彻斯特入学计划”规模（背景化录取、为黑人/白人低参与社区学生提供定向支持），签署“独立承诺”（Stand Alone Pledge）以支持与家庭疏远的学生。
- 庇护群体支持：为庇护寻求者提供“第 26 条奖学金”（Article 26 Scholarships），向寻求庇护者和难民开放图书馆资源。

### （二）学生学业成功支持
- 学习支持：扩大“我的学习必备技能”（MLE）和同伴支持项目（PASS）规模，重点关注通勤学生（调整课程安排）。
- 教学创新：成立曼彻斯特教学与学习学院（MITL），启动“课程改革项目”（聚焦包容性、就业能力和身心健康）。
- 心理健康支持：2019 年启动为期两年的“大曼彻斯特学生心理健康中心”项目，为心理健康障碍和社会沟通障碍学生提供校内专业导师支持。
- 经济支持：“曼彻斯特助学金”（年收入低于 2.5 万英镑的学生每年 2000 英镑，2.5 万-3.5 万英镑的学生每年 1000 英镑），为脱离监护学生和与家庭疏远的学生提供困难补助。

### （三）毕业发展支持
- 就业支持：提供实习助学金（最高 1000 英镑）、残疾学生专属实习项目、“曼彻斯特黄金导师计划”（Manchester Gold）及“全球毕业生计划”（Global Graduates）。
- 研究生入学拓展：推出“曼彻斯特硕士助学金”（3000 英镑），将英格兰高等教育资助委员会（OfS）“消除成功障碍”项目成果融入日常工作。

## 五、学生咨询与治理
- 与学生联盟（UMSU）建立紧密合作：学生联盟代表参与关键委员会（入学与参与战略小组、规划与资源委员会），共同打造多项举措（如少数族裔包容学生大使计划）。
- 组织多元学生焦点小组参与战略制定；计划完善 LGBTQ+ 学生数据收集与支持服务。

## 六、评估与监控
- 由“入学与参与监控评估小组”（APME）负责监督。
- 为核心项目制定“变革理论”，实现数据关联（如毕业发展数据与参与项目挂钩），通过高等教育入学追踪系统（HEAT）进行纵向追踪。
- 每年发布《拓展参与年度报告》，将入学与参与计划（APP）目标纳入学校年度绩效评估（APR）。

## 七、财务投入
- 年度总投入（入学支持、经济支持、研究与评估）保持在高等教育学费收入（HFI）的 25% 左右（2020-2025 学年）。
- 关键投入分配：2020-21 学年经济支持 1168 万英镑，入学活动 353 万英镑，研究与评估 87.5 万英镑。

## 八、核心挑战与机遇
- **挑战**：少数族裔、残疾、低收入学生的学业成就差距持续存在；部分群体规模较小（如脱离监护学生、黑人学生）导致数据波动；通勤学生延续率偏低；心理健康支持存在缺口。
- **机遇**：区域合作项目（大曼彻斯特心理健康中心、大曼彻斯特高等教育联盟）；针对交叉弱势群体的定向项目；课程改革与数字化创新（如“我的曼彻斯特”平台）；与学生联盟的紧密合作及学生共创模式。
